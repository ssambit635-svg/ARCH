## 1. Definitions

Terms used but not defined here have the meanings given in the Terms of Service, the
Privacy Policy, or applicable data protection law. In addition:

- **Applicable Data Protection Law** — any law applicable to the processing, including India's
  **Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023** ("DPDP Act"), and, where applicable, the EU/UK
  **GDPR**.
- **Personal Data** — any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person
  processed by ARCH on behalf of the Customer under the Terms.
- **Data Subject** — the individual to whom Personal Data relates.
- **Processing** — any operation performed on Personal Data, as defined in Applicable Data Protection
  Law.
- **Subprocessor** — a third party engaged by ARCH to process Personal Data on the Customer's behalf.
- **Security Incident** — a breach of security leading to accidental or unlawful destruction, loss,
  alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to, Personal Data.

Where the DPDP Act applies, the Customer is the **Data Fiduciary** and ARCH is the **Data Processor**;
where GDPR applies, the Customer is the **Controller** and ARCH is the **Processor**.

## 2. Roles and scope

1. The Customer determines the purposes and means of processing and is responsible for the lawfulness
   of the Personal Data it submits.
2. ARCH processes Personal Data only:
   a. in accordance with the Customer's documented instructions (the Terms, this DPA, the features the
      Customer enables, and written support requests);
   b. to provide, secure, support and maintain the Service;
   c. as required by applicable law — in which case ARCH will inform the Customer before processing,
      unless legally prohibited.
3. ARCH will notify the Customer without undue delay if, in its opinion, an instruction infringes
   Applicable Data Protection Law, and may decline to follow it.
4. **ARCH does not:**
   - sell Personal Data or share it with advertisers;
   - use Personal Data for its own marketing purposes;
   - train machine-learning models on Personal Data or Customer Content;
   - process Personal Data for any purpose beyond providing the Service.

The subject matter, duration, nature, purpose and categories of processing are described in
**Annex 1**.

## 3. Customer responsibilities

1. ensures it has a lawful basis to submit Personal Data to the Service, including any necessary

4. uses the in-product export and deletion features for its own Data Subject requests, requesting

## 4. Confidentiality of personnel

ARCH ensures that personnel authorised to process Personal Data: (a) are bound by written
confidentiality obligations; (b) receive appropriate data protection and security training; and
(c) are granted access on a least-privilege, need-to-know basis, with production access granted

or where legally compelled, and is recorded in ARCH's internal audit trail.

ARCH may update these measures provided the overall level of protection is not reduced. ARCH is not
responsible for security incidents caused by the Customer's own misconfiguration (for example,

Taking into account the nature of the processing, ARCH will assist the Customer with appropriate
technical and organisational measures in responding to Data Subject requests, including by providing:
in-product **access and export** (incidents, timeline, audit logs); **rectification** through normal

1. ARCH will not respond to a Data Subject request relating to Customer Personal Data directly, unless
   legally required or instructed by the Customer.
2. If ARCH receives such a request, it will notify the Customer without undue delay and will not
   disclose Personal Data in response without the Customer's instruction, unless required by law.

## 8. Security Incident notification

1. ARCH will notify the Customer **without undue delay and in any event within 72 hours** of becoming
   aware of a Security Incident affecting Customer Personal Data.
2. The notification will include, to the extent known: the nature of the incident; the categories and
   approximate volume of Personal Data and Data Subjects affected; the likely consequences; the
   measures taken or proposed to address it; and the contact point for information.
3. ARCH will take reasonable steps to contain, investigate, mitigate and prevent recurrence, and will
   provide a written follow-up with root cause and remediation within **5 business days** of the
   initial notification.
4. **Costs of notification and remediation caused by ARCH's breach of this DPA are borne by ARCH.**
5. ARCH will not make public statements about a Security Incident affecting the Customer without the
   Customer's prior agreement, except where required by law or to protect other affected customers.

## 10. Audits and information

2. Not more than **once per 12 months** (unless required by a supervisory authority or following a

   hours; scoped to the Service and this DPA; subject to confidentiality; arranged at least **30
   days** in advance; and performed by an independent auditor bound by confidentiality. **Costs are
   borne by the Customer**, except where material non-compliance is found.
3. ARCH may provide a recognised third-party audit report or certification in place of an on-site
   audit, where available. Where no such report exists, ARCH will respond to a reasonable security

3. On written request, ARCH will certify deletion in writing.
4. Backups are retained on a rolling **[30]-day** cycle and are deleted as they age out. Backup data is

# Annex 2 — Technical and organisational measures

**Access control.** Role-based access (OWNER/ADMIN/RESPONDER/VIEWER) enforced server-side on every
mutation; organization derived from the authenticated session, never from client input; single-use
invitations; least-privilege production access granted per person and logged; quarterly access reviews.

**Tenant isolation.** Every tenant-owned row carries `organizationId`; repository-layer queries take
`organizationId` as a required parameter; foreign-resource requests return 404 (no existence
disclosure); isolation is enforced by an automated test suite that must pass before any deployment.

**Encryption.** TLS 1.2+ for all data in transit with HSTS; encryption at rest for the database and
backups; secrets stored only as hashes or in a secrets manager, never in code or logs.

**Application security.** TypeScript strict mode; schema validation (Zod) on every request body, query
parameter and webhook payload; HMAC verification of inbound webhooks **before** parsing; replay
protection via timestamp freshness; parameterised database access only; OWASP Top 10 reviewed;
dependency scanning in CI that fails on high/critical findings; secrets scanning pre-commit.

**Logging and monitoring.** Structured application logs with PII scrubbing; error tracking; alerts on
authentication anomalies, webhook rejection spikes, error-rate changes and resource exhaustion; no
credentials, tokens or hashes in logs.

**Audit.** Immutable audit log of every mutation (actor, action, entity, timestamp, metadata) written
in the same transaction as the change; no product path to edit or delete audit entries; exportable by
the Customer.

**Availability and resilience.** Automated daily encrypted backups with **[30]**-day retention and
point-in-time recovery where supported; quarterly restore rehearsals; documented rollback procedure;
external uptime monitoring; a published status page for ARCH itself.

**People.** Written confidentiality obligations; security training; documented incident-response
runbook; blameless post-mortems with tracked action items; no production data used in development.

**Vulnerability management.** Dependency audits on every build; monthly patching cycle; immediate
handling of critical CVEs; annual penetration test (from v1.1) or following a severe incident;
coordinated disclosure policy in [SECURITY.md](../../SECURITY.md).
